About

  • 🌏

    r @ y Olguin is a Research Engineering Technician with the High Resolution Imaging Science Experiment (HiRISE) at the University of Arizona’s Lunar and Planetary Laboratory (LPL). At HiROC, the operations center for NASA’s HiRISE camera on the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO), he produces HiRISE and CTX DTMs, leads the HiRISE XR Development Program, and helps validate planetary datasets for NASA’s PDS with senior scientists at PSI.

  • 🌗

    From photon to pixel to planet: he builds high–resolution Digital Terrain Models of Mars through stereo alignment, bundle adjustment, orthorectification, and MOLA geodetic control, with sub–meter accuracy that supports surface–change studies, rover traverse planning, and mission design.

  • ⚡

    He is pursuing a B.S. in Electrical and Computer Engineering with an electrical engineering focus and minors in Planetary Science, Mathematics, Computer Engineering, and Italian. Training in project management, architectural and landscape design, and 3D/XR visualization places him at the intersection of satellite systems engineering and planetary science.

A Note on Exploration

That goal is not abstract. It was built one late night at a time, at a SOCET SET workstation, watching Mars resolve into terrain. In his own words:

"The most powerful systems I've worked on don't run on voltage or photon flux. They run on human curiosity. And America, at its best, has always been the place that refuses to waste it."
I've spent thousands of hours inside that pipeline. Extracting sub-pixel elevation data from Martian terrain, validating geodetic control against MOLA, delivering elevation models that support rover EDL targeting at NASA scale. Sitting alone at that workstation at 2am, watching a digital elevation model of Mars render line by line, I've felt something that has no engineering term for it. The weight of everyone who made that moment possible. Everyone who was told they didn't belong in that room and showed up anyway.
Read the full note
Every one of them sharpened the instrument. Every perspective we almost lost made the science smaller. Every engineer we found in a place nobody thought to look moved the mission forward.
And America keeps finding them. That is not accident. That is who we are at our best.
Europa Clipper is in flight right now. Artemis is building toward the Moon. The power architectures and flight instrumentation being designed in labs like mine will define the next fifty years of planetary science and the first chapter of permanent human presence beyond this world.
That story is not finished. It is accelerating.
The next generation of American engineers will carry it forward. All of them. From every corner of this country, every background, every frequency this nation holds. Not because it is the right thing to do. Because incomplete teams produce incomplete science, and we have too much left to discover to leave anyone behind.
The universe doesn't draw borders.
The best version of America never did either.
And the most powerful thing we will ever launch into deep space is the full signal. Every one of us, pointed at the same horizon.

EC Research Engineering Tech, Topographic Modeling & Engineering Simulation – NASA Europa Clipper
LPL · Mission: Europa Clipper · Target: Europa · Science Team: Dr. Sarah S. Sutton, Dr. Paul Hayne
Project: Topographic Modeling to Simulate Multi–Scale Roughness for Europa Clipper Investigations and Science
Program
Work–MoRE
NASA · Cycle A, 2025–2027
Toolchain
6 tools

30 weeks · Feb–Sep 2026

Objective. Turn Galileo DEMs into controlled test scenes with synthetic multiscale roughness, where every feature’s true shape, size, position, and georeferenced location is known in advance both vertically and horizontally, with EMILIA–3D’s stereo and thermal imaging of lunar regolith as an analog case, so surface–detection and roughness–recovery methods can be validated against ground truth.

6–tool terrain toolchain
01 Preprocessing

Convert georeferenced DEM rasters into Blender–ready meshes while preserving data gaps and measuring each terrain’s native roughness before any synthetic features are added.

02 DEM_Crop

Extract a parametric working window of any size, position, and rotation from a large DEM by ray projection, with full provenance stored on the output.

03 Ridges

Emplace single and double Europa ridges directly on terrain using Head (1999) cross–section profiles and FWHM widths.

04 Europa’s Lenticulae

Populate a DEM with pits and domes following Pappalardo (1998) morphometry, as a statistical scatter or as exactly specified targets.

05 Plumes

Model cryovolcanic plume deposit fields with radial, directional, or multi–lobed spread, finite range, and power–law clast sizing that decays with distance from the vent (still in development).

06 Roughness

Scatter or excavate instanced hemispheres, pyramids, tetrahedra, and convex polytopes with independent control of density, coverage, size, height, and orientation, reporting realized versus requested populations against a 5M face computational budget.
